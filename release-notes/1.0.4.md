SpotNav 1.0.4 adds Great Britain. Install `spotnav-1.0.4.apk` over the existing app; settings, held prices and paired chargers are kept.

## What changed

- Great Britain's fourteen Octopus Agile regions (GB A to GB P) can be chosen as price areas. They are listed under Great Britain, for example "GB C – London", and "Find my region" finds yours from a postcode. The postcode is sent only to Octopus Energy, never to the SpotNav relay, and it is not saved.
- The app reads the price relay's contract v2 area list and index, and falls back to v1 for a relay without them. Prices already held on the phone are kept.
- A day is shown in the area's own time even when the relay's day files follow another calendar: a London day is cut from two Paris market days, and a Lisbon day from two Madrid days. Each part keeps its own exchange rate, tomorrow appears once its main file is published, and the 23- and 25-hour clock-change days come out right.
- Half-hour prices are planned as quarter-hours, and the 15-minute view shows each half-hour as two equal quarters.
- An area whose published price already includes VAT, taxes or fees shows those as locked and "Included in the price", and nothing is added for them. Agile's unit rate is all-in, so a plan's cost is what you pay per kWh.
- The price settings name where the selected area's prices come from, for example Octopus Energy (Agile) or the ENTSO-E Transparency Platform.
- In Great Britain a plan's cost is written as pounds ("£1.33") and its range in miles.
- Matches Home Assistant integration 1.8: a paired charger reads the market's calendar, what its price includes and its source, locks included parts in the price dialog and never sends them back as edits. An older Home Assistant works as before.
- The charger card is tidier: the line under the status shows only the charger's connection state, the charging current row names its phases ("Charging current · 3 phases", with "(the car)" when the car's onboard charger is what limits them), and the month's charge history fits on one line ("56.1 kWh in Oct").
- The energy slider moves in half kWh, so a value like 17.5 kWh set in Home Assistant is held exactly, and the note about values a control could not hold is gone.
